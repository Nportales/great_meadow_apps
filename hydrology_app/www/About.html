Data Details

This dashboard displays hydrographs and water level statistics produced from hourly water level data collected by staff with Acadia National Park (ACAD) and the Northeast Temperate Network (NETN) Inventory and Monitoring Program at permanent well monitoring sites during the growing season (May 14th to October 1st). For more details on data collection methodology, see Miller et al. 2021 and Mittelhauser 2016.

Monitoring Sites Map

Interactive map of water-level monitoring sites in Great Meadow and Gilmore Meadow. Click a site to view a pop-up with additional site information.

Hydrographs

  • Hydrographs show water levels (cm) recorded during the growing season (~ May through October) derived from hourly water level data.
  • The brown horizontal line at 0 cm represents ground/surface level. The blue line represents hourly precipitation in cm.
  • Hydrographs can be generated for individual monitoring sites or for whole-wetland averages. Use the hydrograph controls to select either specific sites or wetland-wide averages. The resulting plot displays colored lines representing hourly water levels recorded at each selected site or averaged across the wetland.
  • When selecting multiple sites for the same year, the water level data for each site will appear overlaid on the same graph as different colored lines. When selecting multiple years for the same site, graphs for each year will stack on top of each other.
  • Data can be selected using the brush tool (+) by clicking and dragging with your cursor to highlight the section of the hydrograph for which you wish to see the detailed data. Once highlighted on the hydrograph, the corresponding data table will appear in the “Selected Data from Hydrograph” section below.
  • Selected hydrographs can be downloaded as .png images. Selected hydrology data from the hydrographs can be downloaded as .csv datasets.

Water Level Statistics

  • Statistics are calculated from hourly water level data recorded during the growing season (~ May through October).
  • Stat definitions:
    • Mean Water Level (cm): average water level in cm during the growing season.
    • SD Water Level (cm): standard deviation in cm.
    • Minimum Water Level (cm): minimum water level in cm during the growing season.
    • Maximum Water Level (cm): maximum water level in cm during the growing season.
    • Maximum hourly increase (cm): maximum increase in water level within an hour during the growing season.
    • Maximum hourly decrease (cm): maximum decrease in water level within an hour during the growing season.
    • Growing Season Change (cm): change in growing season water level in cm by comparing the average water levels for June and September.
    • *Growing Season Surface Water (%): the percent of the growing season that water level was above the surface.
    • *Growing Season Saturated Soil (%): the percent of the growing season that water level was within 30 cm of the surface.
    • *Growing Season Unsaturated Soil (%): percent of the growing season that water level was below 30 cm of the surface.

*These stats represent thresholds that drive vegetation patterns in wetlands: prolonged surface water often selects for emergent vegetation and when soils remain saturated within the top 30 cm, wetland plant species are favored over upland species. If water level remains below 30 cm of the surface for a prolonged period of time, the wetland stops functioning as a wetland. Please see Miller and Weed 2017 for further details.

Plots:

  • Points show annual means ± standard error (SE) for each statistic across all sites within each wetland over time. Please note that Gilmore Meadow has one water level monitoring site and therefore no SE bars. Dashed lines represent the grand means for each wetland across all years.
  • Statistics can be displayed with data from wetlands individually or together for comparison. When both wetlands are chosen, statistical significance is calculated with significant statistics highlighted in yellow reflecting a significant difference between wetlands (p < 0.05). P-values are always provided whether the statistic is significant or not.
  • Plots can be downloaded as .png images.

Tables:

  • Tables show water level statistics and can be summarized in four different ways:
    • Site per Year: Each site shown for each year individually.
    • Site Averaged Across Years: Each site averaged across all selected years.
    • Wetland per Year: Each wetland shown for each year (averaged across all sites selected within each wetland).
    • Wetland Averaged Across Years: Each wetland averaged across all years (averaged across all sites selected within each wetland). If four or more years of data are selected, statistical significance will be calculated comparing averages between wetlands. Significant statistics are highlighted in yellow reflecting a significant difference between wetlands (p < 0.05) and the associated p-value is provided.
  • Tables can be downloaded as .csv datasets.
